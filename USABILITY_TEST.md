# Task-based usability protocol

## Status

This document is a protocol, not a completed user study. Do not report results
until participants complete the tasks and the raw observations are recorded.

## Participants

Recruit at least five people who did not build the interface. Include different
roles when possible: resident or local worker, visitor, planner/design student,
and a participant with limited visualization experience. Record role, device,
browser, language, and consent; do not collect unnecessary personal data.

## Tasks

1. Complete the first-use language and evidence-boundary flow. Explain in your
   own words what a high score does and does not mean.
2. Capture the lotus-facing frame near 107° at a narrow field of view. Identify
   one candidate supported by both local contrast and reference novelty.
3. Open the local adjacency network. Hover a node and identify the exact photo
   region it represents. Explain what creates an edge.
4. Open the reference matching network. Explain what the line means and what
   line length does not mean.
5. Capture a wider or different-direction frame. Compare results and explain
   why the candidate order may change.
6. Open Learning & Evidence. Determine whether PatchCore is implemented.

## Measures

- task completion without help (yes/no);
- time per task;
- errors or misleading interpretations;
- whether the participant correctly distinguishes all three scores;
- whether the participant correctly states node and edge definitions;
- whether the participant recognizes framing, weather, and reference-set limits;
- one short confidence rating (1–5) plus an open comment after each task.

## Success criteria for the next revision

- at least 4/5 participants correctly explain the claim boundary;
- at least 4/5 distinguish local, reference, and combined scores;
- at least 4/5 link a network node to the highlighted photo region;
- no participant concludes that PatchCore is currently implemented;
- every observed failure becomes a tracked interface or documentation change.
